In one line

Every number on this site is a projection of points, and every recommendation is the same comparison: what does this change buy me over the next few gameweeks, and what does it cost.

Nothing here is a forecast of what will happen. A projection is the mean of a wide distribution, and a real gameweek lands somewhere in that distribution rather than on the mean. The page is worth using to rank options against each other, not to predict a score.

Expected points

Per player, per gameweek. Written out of the actual scoring rules rather than fitted as a black box.

xP = P(appears)          x appearance points
   + expected goals      x points per goal for the position
   + expected assists    x 3
   + P(clean sheet)      x clean sheet points for the position
   + saves / 3                        (goalkeepers)
   - expected goals conceded / 2      (goalkeepers and defenders)
   + expected bonus
   + P(defensive contribution) x 2

Each rate comes from that player's own per-90 statistics this season, then three things are done to it.

Thin samples are pulled towards a price-conditional prior

Three games of football is not enough evidence to trust a rate. A striker with one goal from a single substitute appearance reads as a 1.0 expected-goals-per-90 player, and a model that believes him will recommend him.

So each rate is blended with a prior, weighted by minutes played:

used = w x (player's own rate) + (1 - w) x prior
w    = minutes / (minutes + 270)

At 270 minutes, three full matches, the two weigh equally. By a full season the prior is almost irrelevant.

The prior is conditioned on price, not on position. Shrinking everyone towards "an average forward" is a systematic penalty on exactly the players people captain, because it pulls a genuine outlier towards a number already known to be false about him. Within each position the rate is regressed on cost, so a 15.5m forward is compared against what 15.5m forwards do. Price is FPL's own encoding of expectation, set from last season before a ball was kicked this one.

Fixtures scale the output

FPL publishes a difficulty rating from 1 to 5 for every fixture. Attacking output, clean-sheet probability and goals conceded are each scaled by it, with a small home adjustment. A blank gameweek is an empty fixture list and scores zero; a double is two fixtures and the terms simply add, which is the whole reason doubles are worth planning around.

The immediate gameweek is blended with FPL's own estimate

FPL publishes ep_next, its own expected points for the coming gameweek. For that one gameweek the two are blended 65 to 35 in favour of this model. Two mediocre estimates of the same quantity beat either alone, and FPL's sees team news this model cannot.

What a squad is worth

This is the number behind the wildcard panel, and the one most often misread.

squad score = sum over each gameweek in the horizon of
                ( best starting eleven's xP
                + the captain's xP again
                + 0.12 x the bench's xP )

Three things follow from that, and they explain why the figure is larger than a gameweek score.

It covers the whole horizon, four gameweeks by default. A squad score near 310 is roughly 78 per gameweek, not 310 in a week.

The captain is counted twice, because the armband doubles those points. An eleven projected at 68.6 becomes 76.6 once the captain is added again.

The bench is weighted at 0.12, neither zero nor one. At zero the optimiser fills all four bench slots with the cheapest players in the game, since they never score, and calls that efficient. The weight adds roughly 2 points per gameweek to every squad, so it barely moves comparisons, but it does inflate the absolute figure.

All three figures on the wildcard panel come from this one function applied to three different squads, which is what makes the difference between them a fair comparison even though the absolute level is model-specific.

Transfers and the points hit

Where most of the disagreement with a gut call comes from.

net = points gained over the horizon
    - 4 x (transfers beyond your free ones)

A hit is judged over four gameweeks, not one. Over a single gameweek almost no hit ever pays; over a season almost every hit does. Four gameweeks is roughly how long a transfer's edge survives before form, fixtures and prices move on.

A move is scored on what it does to your squad, not to the player slot. Upgrading a bench goalkeeper looks like a large gain on raw projections and is worth almost nothing in points. Candidates are shortlisted on the raw difference, then rescored by rebuilding the eleven around the change.

A hit must clear the best free plan by 2 points before it is recommended. The hit is a certain loss bought with an estimated gain, and the estimate is nowhere near precise enough to justify the trade on a hair. Without this the tool once recommended paying 4 certain points for an edge of 0.08.

Free transfers roll over up to five, so an unused one is not wasted and "roll it" is a real recommendation.

Chips

What each is worth is arithmetic. Whether to play it is a judgement, and the difference is stated.

Triple Captain  = the captain's xP        (a third copy)
Bench Boost     = the four bench players' xP
Free Hit        = best possible eleven that week, less your own
Wildcard        = squad score of an optimal rebuild, against the
                  best ordinary plan rather than against standing still

The wildcard is compared against the best transfer plan, not against doing nothing. Almost any wildcard beats doing nothing; the question is whether it beats what free transfers and a sensible hit would have achieved anyway. It has to win by 8 points before the recommendation flips, because the chip is a one-shot resource.

The other three are valued in every gameweek of the horizon, and are only suggested when this gameweek is the best in view and the value clears a floor. That is why a chip worth 13 points now can still come back as "hold": if it is worth 15 in two weeks, the sparkline shows you.

What this cannot see

The limits are part of the method, not a footnote to it.

Team news beyond the flags. A fit player who has quietly lost his place still projects as a starter until the minutes data catches up. Press conferences, tactical shifts and late fitness tests are invisible.

Anything beyond the horizon. Chip values cover the gameweeks in view and no further. Double gameweeks are announced when cup fixtures are rearranged, often long before the schedule this reads reflects them, so a better week later cannot be ruled out.

Your purchase prices. Selling price is taken as the current price. FPL only exposes true selling prices to the logged-in manager, so a squad sitting on price rises has slightly more money than this assumes.

Interactions between features. The projection adds independent terms. It does not know that a cheap defender in a good defence and a good fixture is worth more than the two effects separately.

Nothing here is betting advice, and none of it should be read as certainty.

Where the data comes from

The public Fantasy Premier League API, unauthenticated. Player rates, prices, ownership, availability and club news from bootstrap-static; opponent, venue and difficulty from fixtures; your squad, bank and chip history from the entry endpoints. Squad size, the three-per-club cap, the budget, the sell-on fee and both wildcard windows are read from the live game configuration rather than typed in, so they cannot drift out of date here without drifting in the real game.

Responses are cached on disk, an hour for the player list and fixtures and two minutes for anything manager-specific. Current freshness is at /api/health.
